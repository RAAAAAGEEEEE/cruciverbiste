Writing rules (all mandatory)

1. Natural language, crossword-setter tone: one short sentence, 80 characters at most, no final period, no em dash (use a comma or a colon).
2. Never the answer, nor a word of its family, nor a fragment of it, nor a transparent translation.
3. One possible answer for the given domain and letter count. If another well-known word of the same length could fit, disambiguate ("in the original", "in the TV adaptation").
4. Only facts you are certain of. If in doubt about a fact, a name or its official spelling, give up on that entry (answer "skip"). No date, figure or superlative (first, only, last, biggest) without absolute certainty. Nothing that changes over time ("currently", "recent", scores, player counts).
5. Same part of speech as the answer: a noun takes a noun phrase, a verb takes a verb phrase, a plural a plural.
6. Place the subject (work, universe, field) without naming the answer. Avoid vague formulas such as "a famous character".
7. No spoilers about a work's ending in a level 1 or 2 clue.
8. One idea per clue; each clue takes a different angle from the other clues of the same entry. Two clues that differ by a single synonym are one clue.
9. Wordplay only if honest: the literal reading stays true and leads to the right answer. A fair misdirection fools the first reading but is true on the second; an unfair one (false fact, nonexistent sense) is forbidden.
10. No reference to identifiable real people and no shocking content, unless the project says otherwise.

Difficulty scale
- Level 1, "door": direct and accessible, often a fill-in-the-blank ("___ of the Triforce"), or the category noun plus one distinctive trait. Solved in seconds by anyone who knows the subject. No trap.
- Level 2, "middle": a precise cultural fact (origin work, role, mechanic, localized or original name, link to another element). One reading, one answer.
- Level 3, "wall": allusive, through image, analogy, double meaning or fair misdirection, but only one possible answer once length and domain are taken into account. The solver should say "of course" on discovering it.

Devices at your disposal (vary them from one clue to the next)
- fill-in-the-blank; - synonym or hypernym plus a distinctive trait; - precise fact; - analogy or image; - honest double meaning; - fair misdirection; - relation ("rival of...", "mentor of..."); - scope marker ("in the original", "in the localized version").
